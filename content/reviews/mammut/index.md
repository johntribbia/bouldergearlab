    <h3>Fit and Stability</h3>

    <p>The vest runs snug and athletic. It sits close to the body without compressing, and on uphills where I'm leaning into the grade it stays locked. I've run it over a base layer in the 40s and skin-direct in the 70s with no fit change worth noting.</p>

    <p>Bounce is minimal. I ran the Bear Peak descent at tempo several times with a full load: two 500ml soft flasks, a packable shell, snacks, and a phone. Nothing shifted, nothing slapped. For a vest in this weight class, that's the main thing I needed to verify, and it passed.</p>

    <h3>The 3D Mesh Back Panel</h3>

    <p>The mesh panel creates real separation between fabric and skin. It's not channel-frame ventilation, but there's enough airflow that I noticed it on a July run in direct sun. After a 90-minute loop my jersey was evenly damp rather than the pressure-soaked stripe you get from a flat foam panel. At the summit when the wind hits, drying happens faster. For hot-weather mountain running, this matters.</p>

    <h3>Pocket Layout</h3>

    <p>The two front chest pockets sit in the right position for gel access at pace. You reach them without breaking stride, and they're flat enough not to catch wind on exposed ridge sections. Flask retention uses bungee cord on each pocket; it holds 500ml flasks securely without being a fight to open.</p>

    <p>The back tunnel pocket fits a packable rain jacket cleanly. Access is from the side, so you pull a layer out while moving without removing the vest. On days when afternoon storms built over the Divide, I used this pocket on every outing. It worked as intended every time.</p>

    <p>A zippered main compartment handles a phone, keys, or an emergency bivy. That covers the essentials for a 60 to 90-minute mountain run. Push the outing to three hours or layer up heavily and you'll run short on room. The 12L capacity is honest for its intended use; it's not trying to be a full-day pack.</p>

    <div class="test-note">
      <strong>Capacity Notes from Testing</strong>
      On the shorter Fern Canyon and West Ridge routes (under 90 min), the 12L worked well with no compromises. On the longer Bear Canyon direct approach (2+ hours), I began rationing space. Runners who consistently carry a full layer kit or extra water should size up.
    </div>

    <h3>Durability</h3>

    <p>Twelve outings on rocky terrain, including several scrambling sections where I grabbed the vest, and the ripstop shows no snagging or delamination. The stitching at the shoulder anchor points is clean. Zipper pulls haven't snagged once.</p>

    <div class="pullquote">
      "The vest does what it's built for: keeps a short-outing kit organized, ventilated, and stable at pace. It doesn't try to be more than that."

    <h3>Weight and Stiffness</h3>

    <p>These are light at pickup, light enough that your first instinct is to question whether they'll hold under hard use. They do. The carbon shaft doesn't flex or deflect under aggressive weight bearing. I drove hard into them on steep descents and pushed off them climbing. No structural movement in the shaft at any point across all twelve outings.</p>

    <h3>The Folding System</h3>

    <p>Three sections, push-button release. The fold-down and lock-up are both faster than any twist-lock or lever-lock I've used. On runnable flats I want poles stowed fast and deployed fast. Under ten seconds each way, consistently, with gloves on. The lock clicks home clearly; there's no ambiguity about whether the pole is extended or not.</p>

    <h3>Adjustability</h3>

    <p>Seven length settings cover the full range of what mountain running requires. Shorter on steep ascents, collapsed for scrambling sections, extended long for scree descents where I want more braking leverage. I adjusted through this range on every outing, and the mechanism handled repeated use without any loosening or slipping under load.</p>

    <div class="test-note">
      <strong>Length Changes Per Terrain, Bear Peak</strong>
      Fern Canyon switchbacks (20-25% grade): shortened from flat-terrain length. Summit scramble block: collapsed and stowed. Scree descent off the west ridge: extended to maximum for braking. Each adjustment took under 10 seconds with gloves on.
    </div>

    <h3>EVA Foam Grip</h3>

    <p>The grip absorbs sweat without getting slippery during high-output climbing. It doesn't retain water at stream crossings. Over two-hour outings it stayed comfortable with no hotspots. The grip length works in both a standard hold and a shortened low-grip position for steep ascents, though a slightly longer grip zone would give more range in the low-grip position at full compression.</p>

    <h3>Tips</h3>

    <p>Carbide tips bite cleanly into rock and root. Swapping to rubber for trail or road sections takes about five seconds. Both tip types are included in the box, which removes any guesswork about compatibility.</p>

    <h2>Summary</h2>

    <p>Both pieces do their jobs without friction. The vest fits well, ventilates well, and keeps a short-outing kit organized and stable. The poles are stiff, light, and fast to deploy. Used together on Bear Peak across a range of conditions, neither piece required any management or adjustment I didn't intend to make.</p>

    <p>The vest's 12L capacity is its main constraint. It suits 60 to 90-minute mountain runs with a light kit. Beyond that, runners will want more volume. The poles have no equivalent constraint; they scaled to every terrain type and effort level I tested.</p>

    <div class="verdict">
      <div class="verdict-label">Bottom Line</div>
      <p>Aenergy Trail Vest: Well-built for its purpose. The ventilation, stability, and pocket layout all perform. Right-sized for short mountain outings; limited for longer ones.</p>
      <p>Aenergy Ultra Carbon Poles: The best-performing folding poles I've tested on Bear Peak. Light, stiff, and fast. The adjustability range and fold speed make them practical on technical mountain terrain where conditions change across a single outing. Recommended without qualification.</p>
